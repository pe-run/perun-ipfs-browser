Governments don’t like it when they can’t control you. That’s why it’s getting harder to find hardware and software that truly respect your privacy and don’t track your every move. Whenever an interesting solution gives users more independence, sooner or later it disappears or gets pushed to the margins.

Take Skype, for example. It went from a peer-to-peer messenger to a centralized service and was eventually shut down altogether. Something similar happened to IPFS support in the Brave browser. It was a revolutionary feature that let you use the decentralized web without extra software. Unfortunately, it was removed (under pressure from Big Brother?). Of course, you can still set up IPFS access yourself, but most users either don’t know how or simply don’t want to spend the time.

That’s where the idea for Perun IPFS Browser came from — a browser with native IPFS support that puts decentralization, independence, and user freedom first.

It lets you pin the content you care about and help preserve it. That data can remain available on the network practically indefinitely, as long as at least one device stores and shares it.

What’s more, with Perun Protocol V1, the browser is intended to enable direct communication and publishing without relying on central servers. No single company running the whole platform, no central point of control, and no single switch that can shut down the entire community.

Perun IPFS Browser — the perfect browser for the day of the Apocalypse. :)
